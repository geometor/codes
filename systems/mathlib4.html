Lean 4 Mathlib: Dimension-Polymorphic Affine Torsors¶

  • Repository: _refs/mathlib4 (https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4, Apache-2.0, Mathlib2020)

  • Scale Audited: 46 files (16,844 LOC) in Mathlib/Geometry/Euclidean/ + 24 files (11,708 LOC) in Mathlib/LinearAlgebra/AffineSpace/

  • Mathematical Foundation: Dependent type-theoretic affine torsor NormedAddTorsor V P over a real InnerProductSpace ℝ V

1. Why Points and Vectors Are Separate Types¶

In Cartesian software (sympy.geometry), a point \((x, y)\) and a displacement vector \(\langle dx, dy \rangle\) share coordinates relative to a privileged origin \((0, 0)\). mathlib4 enforces coordinate-free discipline through the typeclass stack:

variable {V : Type*} {P : Type*}
          [NormedAddCommGroup V] [InnerProductSpace ℝ V] [MetricSpace P] [NormedAddTorsor V P]

Here p₁ + p₂ is a compile-time type error! You may only subtract two points to obtain a vector (p₁ -ᵥ p₂ : V), translate a point by a vector (v +ᵥ p : P), or form weighted affine combinations (Finset.centroid, midpoint, AffineMap.lineMap) whose weights sum to 1.

2. Where Affine Torsors Shine: Arbitrary Dimension \(n\)¶

Because V need not have dimension 2, Mathlib/Geometry/Euclidean/ achieves breathtaking generality across dimensions:

  • Monge Point & :math:`3(n+1)`-Point Sphere (`MongePoint.lean`, `NinePointCircle.lean`): Instead of proving altitude concurrency only for planar triangles, Simplex.mongePoint generalizes the orthocenter to an arbitrary \(n\)-simplex in \(\mathbb{R}^n\), and NinePointCircle.lean proves Feuerbach’s nine-point circle as the \(n\)-dimensional :math:`3(n+1)`-point sphere!

  • Directed Twice-Angles Modulo :math:`2pi` (`Angle/Oriented/Basic.lean`): When [Module.Oriented ℝ V (Fin 2)] and [Fact (Module.finrank ℝ V = 2)] are added for 2D plane geometry, oangle x y z (∡ x y z : Real.Angle) lives in \(\mathbb{R} / 2\pi\mathbb{Z}\). Multiplying by (2 : ℤ) • ∡ p₁ p₂ p₃ kills ray-reversal (π becomes 2π = 0), encoding directed line angles cleanly and proving inscribed-angle concyclicity (Sphere/Basic.lean) without case splits!

  • 1-Line La Hire Pole–Polar Reciprocity (`Sphere/PolePolar.lean`): Defines Sphere.power s p := dist p s.center ^ 2 - s.radius ^ 2 and Sphere.polar s p := mk' (inversion s.center s.radius p) (ℝ ∙ (p -ᵥ s.center))ᗮ, proving Philippe de La Hire’s reciprocity q ∈ s.polar p ↔ p ∈ s.polar q in a handful of inner-product rewrite steps across all dimensions!

  • Compositional Naming Grammar: Theorems read like self-documenting sentences (conclusion_of_hypothesis, e.g., mul_dist_add_mul_dist_eq_mul_dist_of_cospherical in Sphere/Ptolemy.lean).

3. Forensic Blind Spots: Noncomputability, Cyclic Order & Projective Gaps¶

  1. Pervasive ``noncomputable`` Definitions: Simplex.circumsphere uses Classical.choose on an existence proof, and EuclideanGeometry.angle invokes noncomputable Real.arccos. You cannot execute a mathlib4 Euclidean construction to inspect exact symbolic coordinates!

  2. No Constructive ``Euclid I.1`` & Awkward Cyclic Order: Because two spheres in \(\mathbb{R}^3\) meet in a circle rather than two points, mathlib4 has no planar compass-and-straightedge Euclid I.1 theorem. Moreover, Sphere/Ptolemy.lean:22-41 contains a candid 20-line ``TODO`` block lamenting the lack of a clean cyclic-order predicate on circles in vector-torsor geometry.

  3. Wide-Open Projective & Coaxial Omissions: Despite defining Projectivization K V in LinearAlgebra/Projective/Basic.lean, mathlib4 currently has zero radicalAxis, zero crossRatio, zero Menelaus (despite having Ceva.lean!), zero Pappus, zero Desargues, zero general conics in Geometry/Euclidean/, and zero geometric theorems on Real.goldenRatio or Platonic solids.

4. Takeaway for GEOMETOR¶

Adopt mathlib4’s conclusion_of_hypothesis naming discipline and its crystal-clear power / inversion / polar conceptual factoring in geometor.elements 2.0 and geometor.model.inversion, while retaining 100% computable ANP coordinates and full projective/golden coverage.
