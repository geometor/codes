The Eight Paradigms of Formal & Visual Geometry¶

Why do eleven major formalizations of Euclidean and projective geometry look as if they come from eleven different planets? Because a formal language for geometry must simultaneously answer four questions that pull in opposite directions:

  1. Ontology — Are lines, circles, and conics primitive types, predicates over points, affine vector cosets, graded Clifford blades, \(3 \times 3\) complex matrices, or inline colored visual glyphs?

  2. Continuity & Root Selection — When two circles intersect, how does the system know an intersection exists, and how does it distinguish the upper pole F from the lower pole E?

  3. Metric Representation — Does the language measure length and angle via real square roots and arccos, synthetic Cong equivalence classes, linear angles mod \(\pi\) and log-ratios, coordinate-free areas \(S_{ABC}\) and Pythagoras difference \(P_{ABC}\), or polynomial quadrance \(Q\) and spread \(s\)?

  4. Epistemic Certificate — Is geometric truth certified by a small dependent-type kernel (Coq, Lean 4), an external SMT solver, sparse Gaussian saturation, polynomial triangulation, randomized complex evaluation, human visual perception, or exact Algebraic Number Polynomial (ANP) canonicalization?

The Noetic Tension: Symbolic Rigor vs. Intuitive Nexus¶

In De Noetica Geometriae (Hoenen1954), Peter H. J. Hoenen observed that geometric knowledge neither floats in pure imageless logic nor reduces to empirical measurement of ink on paper. Rather, human reason grasps universal necessity inside the concrete spatial Nexus of the constructed figure (abstractio formalis intuitiva).

Viewed through Hoenen’s lens, the history of formal geometry over the last century splits into two halves that rarely speak to one another:

  • Imageless Deductive Engines (GeoCoq, mathlib4) achieve unassailable logical certainty inside a proof assistant’s type kernel, yet sever the link to executable coordinates and visual diagrams: mathlib4 cannot compute the coordinates of an equilateral apex without noncomputable choice, and GeoCoq dissolves lines and circles into six-quantifier point formulas.

  • Numeric Sketch Engines (CindyJS, AlphaGeometry’s numericals.py) embrace concrete coordinates to prune branches and draw diagrams, yet fall back to IEEE-754 floating-point approximations (1e-7 tolerances in Newclid, randomized complex floats in CindyJS), losing exact algebraic identity over \(\mathbb{Q}(\phi)\) or nested quadratic towers.

GEOMETOR’s foundational thesis is that exact symbolic algebra (QQ.algebraic_field + closed-form radical-axis intersections + topological root selectors + Rational Trigonometry invariants + interactive .euclid-graphic SVGs) heals this split: the concrete constructed diagram is an exact algebraic object in \(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}^2\), carrying both visual immediacy and deductive necessity.
